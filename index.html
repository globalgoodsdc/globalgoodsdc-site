Why manufacturers and distributors work with us

A good marketplace retailer does three things well. It buys reliably, it sells competently, and it protects the brand it represents.

We buy, and we keep buying

We purchase inventory outright with our own capital. Once a product proves itself, we reorder it, so you see recurring purchase orders rather than a single trial buy.

  • Purchase orders on agreed terms
  • Orders planned around your case packs and minimums
  • Repeat orders as products sell through

We know how Amazon works

We prepare and label products to Amazon's requirements, match each one to the right ASIN and keep catalog details accurate. When something is wrong on a listing, we know how to raise it.

  • FBA preparation, labelling and inbound shipments
  • ASIN identification and catalog accuracy
  • Listing discrepancy and compliance support

We protect what you have built

We source legitimately, keep to the brand terms we agree to, and tell you plainly how your products are doing on Amazon.

  • Bought from manufacturers and their distributors
  • Agreed brand terms and MAP policies respected
  • Clear documentation and prompt replies

What we bring to your brand on Amazon

The work a manufacturer needs from a marketplace retailer, handled directly by the owner who runs our purchasing and Amazon operations.

Wholesale purchasing and distribution

Branded consumer products bought in wholesale quantities with our own capital, with ongoing supplier relationships rather than one-off buys.

Inventory and replenishment

Demand monitoring and forecasting guide each reorder, to help products stay available through seasonal peaks.

Amazon FBA operations

Products prepared, labelled and shipped into Amazon's fulfillment network to its requirements in Canada, and now in the United States through USA Goods DC Inc.

Catalog and ASIN management

Each product matched to the correct ASIN and pack size. Catalog errors we find are reported to Amazon and to you.

Brand and listing compliance support

Help identifying and reporting listing discrepancies and product information issues through Amazon's own processes, within what a reseller can do.

Respect for brand policies

Legitimate sourcing and the brand terms we agree to, including reseller and MAP policies.

Data-driven purchasing

Our own analytical software evaluates products, estimates demand and profitability after Amazon fees, and plans replenishment.

Marketplace growth

Steady stock and accurate listings support visibility. Where it genuinely fits, we can coordinate advertising or promotions with you.

Every purchase starts with data

We built our own purchasing software. Before we place an order, it shows us how a product sells, what it earns after Amazon's fees, and how much stock we need to stay available while your next shipment is on its way.

  • Demand estimated from sales history, with seasonal patterns
  • Profitability after Amazon fees, with preparation and freight where known
  • Reorder timing that allows for your lead times
  • Open orders and inbound shipments tracked, so the same stock isn't counted twice

What you can expect from us

The basics of a good wholesale account, done consistently.

Clear purchase orders

Every order is written down: products, quantities, prices and the entity being invoiced.

Payment as agreed

We pay on the terms we accept. If anything needs to change, we raise it before the invoice is due.

Legitimate sourcing

We buy from manufacturers, brand owners and their distributors, and we keep the paperwork.

Respect for your terms

Reseller agreements, territories and MAP policies we agree to are policies we follow.

Complete account applications

We supply the corporate and tax documentation your credit or dealer application requires, and references where our suppliers agree.

Prompt, honest communication

Questions get a direct answer, and we tell you plainly how your products are performing, including when something isn't working.

How a partnership starts

  1. Introduce your line

    Send your catalog or price list with UPCs, case packs, minimums and any reseller or MAP policies.

  2. Open an account

    We complete your account application and agree on terms, lead times and which of our companies you'll invoice.

  3. First purchase order

    We order, receive and prepare the goods, and send them into Amazon's fulfillment network.

  4. Grow the line

    As products sell, we reorder, share what's working and look at more of your range together.

Questions suppliers ask

Something else? Write to us and we'll answer directly.

Do you buy inventory, or sell on consignment?

We buy. Inventory is purchased outright with our own capital, on agreed payment terms.

Which company should we open an account with?

Global Goods DC Inc. for products sold in Canada, and USA Goods DC Inc. for the United States. They are separately incorporated, so each has its own account, purchase orders and invoices.

Do you follow MAP and other brand policies?

Yes. We follow the minimum advertised price policies and other brand terms we agree to. We can't control what other sellers do, but we keep to our own commitments.

Can you help with problems on our Amazon listings?

Often, yes. We can identify listing discrepancies or incorrect product information and report them through Amazon's processes. Amazon makes the final decision, and changes reserved for the brand owner still go through you.

Are you a marketing agency?

No. We are a wholesale customer and an Amazon retailer. Where it fits, we can coordinate advertising or promotions for the products we carry.

Do you work with distributors as well as manufacturers?

Yes. We buy from manufacturers, brand owners and wholesale distributors.

What size are your orders?

Opening orders are sized to your case packs and minimums, and grow as products sell through.

How long have you been operating?

Global Goods DC Inc. began operating in Canada on June 11, 2023. USA Goods DC Inc., our affiliated U.S. company in New Jersey, is newly established.

Can you provide references?

We don't publish our suppliers' names. Once we're in conversation, we can discuss our purchasing history and provide references where our suppliers agree.
